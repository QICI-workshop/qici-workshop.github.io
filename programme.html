Lijian Zhang — Nanjing University
The promise and challenges of photonic quantum metrology
Abstract

Quantum metrology, leveraging quantum resources and manipulation techniques, offers measurement capabilities that surpass the limits of its classical counterpart. It holds the promise of driving key breakthroughs in both fundamental scientific research and practical engineering applications. Consequently, it has emerged as one of the three primary pillars of quantum information technology. Over the past two decades, this field has witnessed remarkable progress, with successful implementations across various physical systems, including photons, atoms, ions, nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers, and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR). Among these, photonic systems serve as a crucial platform for quantum precision measurement due to their inherent advantages, such as long coherence time and strong resistance to environmental disturbance. Furthermore, the rich and easily manipulable degrees of freedom of photons make them highly adaptable to a wide range of precision measurement scenarios. The further advancement of photonic quantum metrology relies on the development of technologies for preparing and detecting quantum probe states, the enhancement of system robustness (i.e., improved noise tolerance), and the exploration of novel measurement methodologies tailored for increasingly complex tasks. This presentation will briefly introduce the fundamental principles of photonic quantum metrology, followed by an overview of our recent research progress in quantum interferometry, weak measurement, and multi-parameter quantum precision measurement.

Jing Yang — Zhejiang University
Quantum Sensing Meets Quantum Chaos
Abstract

I will discuss quantum sensing in the context of quantum chaotic dynamics. I will show how quantum chaos affects quantum noise and how quantum noise can, in turn, be leveraged to reveal signatures of quantum chaos.

In the first part, we will discuss Floquet chaotic dynamics generated by Haar random gates and study the scaling behavior of the average quantum Fisher information (QFI) in quantum sensing protocols. We show that in the asymptotic limit of large local Hilbert space dimension, the average QFI for Floquet random circuits can be effectively described by global random matrix models and scales linearly with the system size. We further show that such a scaling behavior is typical in the asymptotic limit.

In the second part, we will show that how quantum sensing can be leveraged to diagnose quantum chaos. We show that the second order derivatives of the average QFI yields the celebrated spectral form factor (SFF) in quantum chaos. Unlike the SFF itself, QFI is self-averaging, suggesting a lower-noise experimental route to detecting spectral correlations.

Augusto Smerzi — Shenzhen Technology University
Quantum Search and Sensing with an Oracle
Abstract

Quantum computing and quantum metrology are typically regarded as separate branches of quantum technologies. Recent developments are beginning to blur this distinction: an unknown physical signal can directly generate an oracle and become part of a quantum algorithm itself. I will discuss a framework based on coherent quantum histories, in which different sensing evolutions are explored in superposition. The coherent dynamics implements the oracle required for quantum search while retaining metrological information about the underlying physical signal. This opens a route to combining digital quantum search and analog parameter estimation within a single coherent process on a quantum computer.

Huangjun Zhu — Fudan University
Near-Optimal Multiparameter Pure-State Metrology with a Constant Number of Bases
Abstract

Multiparameter pure-state metrology is limited by the incompatibility of measurements optimized for different parameters. We characterize uniform sensitivity by the Fisher floor, the smallest fraction of the quantum Fisher information (QFI) retained over all pure states and parameter directions. We prove that a constant number of fixed orthonormal bases suffices for a nearly optimal Fisher floor, independently of the Hilbert-space dimension dd. With high probability, five independent Haar bases already achieve a positive, dimension-independent floor, and larger constant families achieve approximate universal Fisher symmetry: they retain nearly one half of the QFI in every direction, the largest uniform fraction permitted by measurement incompatibility. The basis count is optimal up to a logarithmic factor in inverse accuracy in the large-dd limit. By contrast, complete mutually unbiased bases, when they exist, minimize mean Fisher anisotropy among mixtures of d+1d+1 bases, yet their Fisher floor is only 1/(d+1)1/(d+1): optimal average balance does not ensure uniform sensitivity.

After postdoctoral research at Perimeter Institute and Cologne Institute for Theoretical Physics, he joined Department of physics, Fudan University in January 2018. His main research interest is quantum information theory, including quantum measurements, quantum characterization, verification, and validation (QCVV), entanglement theory, and blind quantum computation etc.

Jingzheng Huang — Shanghai Jiao Tong University
Enhancing precision and privacy of quantum sensing network via cyclic structure
Abstract

Sensing networks underpin applications ranging from fundamental physics to real-world engineering. Distributed quantum sensing (DQS) can improve measurement performance, but existing protocols typically require multipartite entanglement, which poses substantial challenges for scalable implementation. Moreover, as sensor networks become increasingly interconnected, information sharing among multiple users poses potential risks of information leakage, making privacy protection a critical challenge. To address these challenges, we report two of our recent works: 1) We introduce a DQS protocol based on bidirectional causal routing in a cyclic network, where a single probe sequentially interrogates M independent sensors along two opposite causal routes. By exploiting the noncommutativity between inter-sensor propagation and local sensing operations, the protocol turns propagation from a passive transport process into a source of sensing information, yielding an asymptotic 1/M² scaling of the estimation precision without multipartite entanglement. We experimentally demonstrate the protocol for distributed beam-tilt sensing in a free-space quantum optical network comprising up to 9 sensors, achieving picoradian-level precision in estimating the average tilt angle. 2) We evaluate the maximum information leakage ratio (MILR) for sensing network privacy, and then adding probe squeezing to our cyclic network scheme for privacy enhancement. Here, causal-order switching harnesses intrinsic network propagation dynamics for privacy encoding, while probe squeezing suppresses information leakage through quantum channels and improves global estimation precision. These mechanisms enable an asymptotic exponential enhancement on the privacy with the squeezing level. We experimentally demonstrate this mechanism in distributed beam-tilt sensing using spatially structured light without requiring nonclassical light sources. These results together, identify intrinsic network propagation dynamics as a resource for scalable distributed quantum sensing, which opens a new avenue for continuous-variable sensing networks that combine high precision with enhanced privacy.

Yadong Wu — Shanghai Jiao Tong University
Three Paradigms for Learning Scalable Quantum Systems
Abstract

Efficiently characterizing scalable quantum systems remains a central challenge in quantum science, primarily due to the exponential growth of Hilbert-space dimensionality with system size. Recent advances in artificial intelligence (AI), particularly in high-dimensional pattern recognition and function approximation, offer promising new approaches to addressing this challenge. Depending on how prior knowledge is incorporated and learning architectures are designed, AI-based approaches to quantum system characterization can be broadly classified into three complementary paradigms: machine learning, deep learning, and language models. In this talk, I will introduce these three paradigms and discuss how each contributes to the characterization of scalable quantum systems. Particular attention will be given to two recent research directions: predicting Wigner functions of bosonic quantum systems and predicting classical shadows of many-body quantum states.

14:30–15:30Invited talk
Xiongfeng Ma — Tsinghua University
The Power and Limits of Quantum Measurement: From Local Certification to Complexity-Driven Readout
Abstract

How much can we learn about a many-body quantum system from measurements, and what resources do we need? I will first introduce *localizable quantumness*. Local measurements on most of a system leave robust signatures of its global properties in the ensemble of conditional states on a small remaining subsystem. This lets us certify multipartite entanglement, circuit complexity, quantum magic, and state fidelity using nonadaptive local Pauli measurements, with sample complexity and noise tolerance independent of system size for broad classes of states. I will then discuss the cost of preparing and cloning conditional states. For random circuits and certain strongly interacting dynamics, the gate cost of preparing these states can scale with the full circuit volume, even when measurements and postselection are allowed. Cloning individual states from an unknown ensemble can require exponentially many copies of its purification. Polynomial-size preparation circuits can remove this sample barrier. Under standard complexity assumptions, cloning can still be computationally hard even when the circuit is known. Finally, I will present a hidden-to-visible transition in quantum observation. Below an architecture-dependent depth scale, some pure-state encodings reveal only an exponentially small fraction of their quantum Fisher information to any allowed readout. Above that scale, randomized measurements based on approximate unitary 3-designs recover a constant fraction for any pure-state encoding.

Chenfeng Cao — Freie Universität Berlin and The University of Hong Kong
Measurement and noise as primitives for quantum computation
Abstract

Measurement and noise are usually the enemies of quantum computation: one collapses superpositions, the other destroys coherence. I will argue that both are computational resources, and that much of their power depends on who keeps the record they leave behind. With feedforward on the measurement record, shallow circuits escape their light cone—every n-qubit unitary can be implemented exactly in constant depth—and free fermions, otherwise simulable by determinants, become hard to sample without any interaction. Noise can help even when nobody keeps its record: local dissipation that irreversibly kills entanglement can also revive magic. Keeping that record can change the computational class itself: at a fixed thermal exposure, the same processor is classically simulable or quantum universal depending on which environmental records its controller retains.

Yanglin Hu — The University of Hong Kong
Unbounded separation between definite and indefinite causal order in finite-dimensional quantum metrology
Abstract

Indefinite causal order is known to offer enhancements in quantum metrology, notably including an unbounded advantage in the measurement of a geometric phase of a harmonic oscillator. This advantage, however, is specific to the infinite dimensional setting, and its finite dimensional analogue remains elusive, with recent findings suggesting that advantages in finite dimensions may be fundamentally limited to bounded constant factors. Here we show that, in fact, arbitrarily large advantages arise for finite dimensional systems in the finite sample regime. Specifically, we establish an unbounded separation between definite and indefinite causal order in the estimation of a geometric phase associated to two sets of NN displacements generated by discrete position and momentum operators on a dd-dimensional quantum system: for any given constant RR, there exist values of NN and d=Ω(N2)d=\Omega(N^2) such that a strategy with indefinite order uses an initial probe with RR times less energy than the probe required by every strategy with definite order achieving the same mean squared error, whenever the number of measurement shots ν\nu is bounded as ν=O(exp⁡(πd/16)/poly⁡(d))\nu=\mathcal{O}(\exp(\pi d/16)/\poly(d)). In other words, indefinite order offers an energy saving that grows arbitrarily large with the parameters of the problem. To prove this result, we establish an approximate Weyl relation for discrete Gaussian wavepackets, which is of independent technical interest.

Entong He — The University of Hong Kong
unitaryQIP = unitaryPSPACE
Abstract

Unitary synthesis complexity studies the computational cost of applying a target unitary to an unknown quantum state, extending complexity theory beyond its traditional focus on problems with classical inputs and outputs. Rosenthal and Yuen (ITCS 2022) introduced two complexity classes for unitary synthesis: unitaryQIP\mathsf{unitaryQIP} and unitaryPSPACE\mathsf{unitaryPSPACE} for the unitary families implementable via interactive proofs and in polynomial space, respectively. In this work, we prove the equality unitaryQIP=unitaryPSPACE\mathsf{unitaryQIP}=\mathsf{unitaryPSPACE} and thereby establish a unitary-complexity analogue of QIP=PSPACE\mathsf{QIP} = \mathsf{PSPACE} (Jain, Ji, Upadhyay, and Watrous, J. ACM 2011) and stateQIP=statePSPACE\mathsf{stateQIP}=\mathsf{statePSPACE} (Rosenthal and Yuen, ITCS 2022; Metger and Yuen, FOCS 2023).

To prove unitaryQIP⊆unitaryPSPACE\mathsf{unitaryQIP} \subseteq \mathsf{unitaryPSPACE}, we use an acceptance rigidity lemma derived from the soundness condition to show that the polynomial-space prover constructed for a fixed input in state synthesis (Metger and Yuen, FOCS 2023) implements the target unitary on every input, leading to a polynomial-space simulation of the protocol. For the reverse inclusion, we build on OpenAI's recently announced solution to the unitary synthesis problem (Aaronson and Kuperberg, Theory Comput. 2007), and show that unitaryPSPACE\mathsf{unitaryPSPACE} families can be synthesized in polynomial time with a PSPACE\mathsf{PSPACE} oracle. We then implement the oracle queries using auxiliary states prepared via the statePSPACE⊆stateQIP\mathsf{statePSPACE} \subseteq \mathsf{stateQIP} protocol (Rosenthal and Yuen, ITCS 2022).

Beyond the equality, we show that our protocol for unitaryPSPACE\mathsf{unitaryPSPACE} can be parallelized to constant rounds when the verifier receives polynomially many copies of the input, and we also obtain a complexity-theoretic barrier to proving the existence of EFI\mathsf{EFI} pairs.

Penghui Yao — Nanjing University
A Dichotomy for MIP* in the Presence of Unital Noise
Abstract

How much computational power can two quantum provers retain when their shared entanglement is noisy? I will discuss a complete classification for two-prover, one-round interactive proofs in which the provers share copies of an EPR pair affected by unital qubit noise. The resulting class is NEXP if the shared state is separable or has quantum maximal correlation strictly less than one. If the state is entangled and its maximal correlation is one, the class is RE. I will also describe two ingredients of the proof: a low-degree approximation that preserves positivity and a dimension-reduction technique we call Pauli folding. This is joint work with Yangjing Dong, Zhenyu Jiang, Minglong Qin, and Haigang Zhou.

Nana Liu — Shanghai Jiao Tong University
Bridging quantum thermodynamics and optimization
Abstract

Quantum thermodynamics offers a unifying route to quantum algorithms for semidefinite programming (SDP), linking a central tool of optimization to the physics of thermal states. The connection begins with finding a quantum system’s lowest energy while fixing the expectation values of conserved quantities. This is an SDP over quantum states, even when the conserved quantities are represented by noncommuting observables.

We turn this connection into an algorithmic framework by minimizing instead the free energy, which balances energy and entropy. This yields a concave dual optimization problem in chemical potentials—the parameters enforcing the constraints—with a generalized quantum thermal state (Boltzmann framework) as the optimal state. Standard methods, including stochastic gradient ascent, can optimize these parameters, while the minimum free energy approaches the minimum energy at low temperature. We develop first- and second-order classical and hybrid quantum–classical algorithms based on this formulation, with provable convergence and runtime guarantees.

Building on this Boltzmann framework, we extend the thermodynamic interpretation to broader classes of SDPs in quantum information. Trace-one density operators, measurement operators bounded between zero and the identity, and the unbounded positive semidefinite cone admit different thermodynamic regularizations associated with the Boltzmann, Fermi–Dirac, and Bose–Einstein statistics, respectively. In each case, the regularized dual is concave in the chemical potentials and, under suitable regularity assumptions, the regularized primal optimizer has the corresponding thermal form. The dual gradient and Hessian can be evaluated through thermal expectation values and correlation functions, enabling hybrid quantum–classical algorithms for a wide range of SDPs.

Qisheng Wang — Shanghai Jiao Tong University
A Unified Complexity Framework for Quantum Property Testing
Abstract

We develop a unified framework for analyzing the complexity of quantum property testing through functionals of the form Lϕ(ρ)=tr⁡(ϕ(dρ))/d\mathcal{L}_{\phi}(\rho) = \operatorname{tr}(\phi(d\rho))/d, where ρ\rho is an unknown dd-dimensional quantum state and ϕ\phi is a given function. A master theorem is established that derives sample complexity lower bounds for estimating Lϕ(ρ)\mathcal{L}_\phi(\rho) from properties of ϕ\phi, combining Haar-random moment encoding with moment matching and best polynomial approximation. Corresponding query complexity lower bounds follow from quantum sample-to-query lifting. The framework yields (nearly) tight bounds for a broad class of problems, including entropy estimation (von Neumann, R\'enyi, and Tsallis), closeness estimation (trace distance and Uhlmann fidelity), spectrum estimation, rank testing (operator rank, Schmidt rank, and matrix product states). In summary, our results resolve several open problems and establish the optimality of 37 quantum algorithms since 2015, up to constant or polylogarithmic factors.

Jihun Cha — The University of Hong Kong
Experimental tomography of a quantum process with indefinite input-output direction
Abstract

The input-output direction of quantum devices can be coherently altered by a control qubit, offering advantages in information processing, and new insights into the problem of time symmetry in quantum physics. A paradigmatic instance is the so-called quantum time flip (QTF): here we experimentally implement a QTF device using a displaced Sagnac interferometer. Our configuration enhances interferometric stability, in a spatially compact and cost-effective design, lowering the barrier for experimental research on indefinite causal structures. We validate our single-qubit device across 792 experimental datasets spanning 11 unitary channels. Under the balanced model, the reconstructed total instrument and fitted action on the tested probe unitaries yield fidelities of 94.1% and 94.2%, respectively, which improve to 94.3% and 94.4% under the BS coefficient model. An evaluation of 24 datasets across four representative Pauli channels via an online open-access platform yielded an average fidelity of 95.3%, with a range of 85.5% to 99.2%. These high-fidelity results confirm the successful execution of a compact and robust QTF device.

Zihao Li — The University of Hong Kong
Quantum Nonlinear Properties from a Single Measurement Setting
Abstract

Nonlinear properties of quantum states are essential to quantum information and many-body physics, but assessing them experimentally is challenging, as it typically requires multi-copy operations or a large number of measurement settings. To address this challenge, we develop a universal framework, collision-based nonlinear estimation (CBNE), for efficiently measuring nonlinear quantities of a quantum state ρ\rho, such as the higher-order expectation value tr(Oρt)tr(O\rho^t) for some observable OO, using single-copy randomized measurements. Strikingly, our protocol requires only a single measurement setting, provided that the system dimension is sufficiently large or a few auxiliary qubits are available; this contrasts with the conventional expectation that multiple measurement bases are necessary for nonlinear estimation. In addition, for a fixed moment order tt, CBNE is observable-independent at the experimental stage, allowing the same data to be reused for estimating multiple nonlinear quantities. It further extends to broader tasks, including the estimation of principal component properties and partial-transpose moments of quantum states. Our results provide a practical and scalable route for measuring nonlinear state properties on near-term quantum devices.

17:40–18:00Short talk
Jinge Bao — University of Edinburgh
Optimal Tolerant Testing of Lindbladian Dissipation
Abstract

Quantifying dissipation is essential for controlling quantum noise and characterizing open-system dynamics. In practical experimental settings, residual noise may still persist despite efforts to suppress it, making it important to determine whether the dissipative strength remains within a prescribed tolerance threshold. We study this question for an unknown, time-independent Lindblad generator with bounded strength, where the jump operators are local but with unrestricted overlap, using only memoryless forward-evolution access. The task is to distinguish dissipative strength of at most ε1 from strength of at least ε2, for 0≤ε1<ε2, measured in the canonical dissipator's normalized Frobenius norm. We give an algorithm that solves this task in total evolution time O(ε2/(ε2−ε1)2) for all nontrivial thresholds, with constant success probability, and provide a matching lower bound Ω(ε2/(ε2−ε1)2) that establishes optimality even for adaptive protocols. This extends dissipation testing to the tolerant setting and eliminates the bounded-degree requirement, making the framework applicable to a broader range of experimentally relevant settings.
